Plants4Life — Exploring how IoT can support everyday plant care

Overview
Plants4Life is a research-driven concept exploring how smart devices and a mobile app can simplify plant care for both hobbyists and professionals. Combining desk research with user interviews, we identified key challenges in plant maintenance and barriers to smart technology adoption. My responsibilities included conducting desk research, leading five of the ten user interviews, and co-analyzing findings to formulate product recommendations.

01 — Context

Different plant-care routines, shared daily demands

People care for plants for different reasons—some treat it as a relaxing hobby, while others work with plants professionally. Regardless of experience level, keeping plants healthy requires consistent attention, proper lighting, and knowing how to react when issues arise. At the same time, Internet of Things (IoT) technologies offer new ways to automate routine tasks and monitor plant health remotely. We set out to explore how smart solutions could fit into everyday routines and what prevents people from adopting them.

02 — Problem

Limited knowledge and high barriers to smart adoption

Many plant owners lack the experience needed to diagnose issues, while busy schedules make regular care easy to forget. Although IoT could automate repetitive tasks like watering, potential users often view smart devices as expensive, bulky, or too complicated to set up. We needed to understand what plant owners actually struggle with and how an IoT system could become accessible rather than intimidating.

03 — Process

From desk research and interviews to actionable insights

We began with desk research into existing IoT plant-care solutions and human-plant interactions, which helped us frame our interview guide. Next, we conducted 10 individual interviews across two groups: six hobbyists (ages 20–77) and four plant-industry professionals (ages 34–50). I conducted five interviews independently, and we analyzed all ten conversations together by structuring findings into observations, conclusions, and recommendations.

Decision 1 — pairing automation with remote guidance: Participants wanted to reduce repetitive care tasks while still staying informed. Rather than proposing standalone hardware, we focused on combining automated watering and lighting with a mobile app that provides timely reminders and remote monitoring.

Decision 2 — addressing cost and space misconceptions upfront: The biggest adoption barriers were perceived cost and lack of physical space for extra equipment. Because our desk research showed that compact, affordable solutions already exist, we prioritized transparent communication around device size, actual pricing, and clear setup instructions.

Decision 3 — embedding accessible plant-care knowledge: When plants showed signs of distress, participants relied heavily on searching online for advice. To build user confidence, we decided the ecosystem needed built-in educational materials alongside a moderated forum or assistant for quick troubleshooting.

04 — Solution

Research-backed recommendations for a connected ecosystem

Based on our synthesis, we defined a strategic product direction for Plants4Life—a conceptual ecosystem linking compact IoT sensors and automated devices with a companion mobile app. Key recommended features included:

  • Task automation and remote control: Smart devices to handle routine watering and lighting, managed remotely via mobile.
  • Smart reminders and onboarding: Actionable notifications paired with simple, step-by-step guides to lower the barrier for IoT beginners.
  • Knowledge hub and community: An in-app assistant or moderated forum where users can troubleshoot plant issues and share advice.

05 — Outcome

A validated foundation for future product design

The project delivered a structured set of research-based recommendations defining the core value proposition and feature priorities for Plants4Life. Because this was a four-week exploratory research phase, the project concluded at the recommendation stage without moving into UI design or hardware prototyping.

Our findings also highlighted clear next steps for validation—specifically testing price sensitivity, refining notification frequency, and evaluating ease of use among users with limited technical experience.
