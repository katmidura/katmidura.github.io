KIOSK — Designing a self-service kiosk for university dormitories

Overview
DS Kiosk is a self-service kiosk concept designed to make everyday dormitory services more accessible. It brings selected online and reception-based tasks into one place, allowing students and visitors to complete them independently. My responsibilities included early research, designing the overnight stay flow and its hi-fi interface, creating illustrations, and participating in usability testing.
Role
UX/UI Designer
Timeline
March – June 2024 (15 weeks)
Tools
Figma, FigJam
Team
4 UX/UI Designers
Tags
UX/UI, Graphic Design

01 — Context

Two separate channels for everyday tasks

Dormitory services were split between two channels: an online platform and the reception. Students could reserve study rooms, book laundry slots or report damage online, while tasks such as collecting keys, handling packages or arranging overnight stays required visiting the reception. These channels operated separately, making some everyday processes less convenient.

02 — Problem

Services that weren't always accessible

The lack of connection between online and reception-based services made everyday tasks harder to manage. Processes also differed between dormitories, making them less predictable for students. Meanwhile, receptionists had to handle a wide range of requests in person. We saw an opportunity to bring selected services together in a self-service kiosk.

03 — Process

From existing routines to a self-service experience

Our early exploration included informal conversations with people familiar with university dormitories and observations of existing kiosks in shopping centres and a capsule hotel. These activities helped us explore everyday tasks, potential use cases and existing self-service patterns. The interviews were informal and involved a small number of participants, so we treated them as early observations rather than representative research.

Decision 1 — prioritizing the overnight stay flow: As one of the key kiosk functionalities, the overnight stay process became my main design responsibility. After mapping the flow together as a team, I prepared the final wireframes in Figma, focusing on clear instructions, visual guidance and easy navigation between steps.

Decision 2 — making visitor access more independent: We introduced a QR code that residents could generate through the DSNet platform and send to their expected visitors. Visitors could then scan the code at the kiosk and complete the process without requiring the resident to visit the reception. The code could also be reset if it reached an unauthorized person.

Decision 3 — refining the interface through testing: We tested the hi-fi prototype with three participants. Based on their feedback, we improved purchase confirmation, button visibility and document verification, and removed unnecessary navigation from the final screen.

04 — Solution

One kiosk, multiple dormitory services

DS Kiosk brought six main functionalities together in a single touchscreen interface, including overnight stays, package handling, key and vacuum cleaner collection, parking ID purchases, and damage reporting and payments.

My main contribution was the overnight stay flow and its hi-fi interface. I also created 18 custom illustrations to give the interface a friendlier and more consistent visual character. The team prepared reusable components to maintain consistency across screens. The visual direction was inspired by the identity of AGH dormitories.

05 — Outcome

A more independent dormitory experience

The project resulted in a high-fidelity kiosk concept combining several dormitory services in one place. The overnight stay flow explored a more independent visitor experience through QR code verification, while usability testing helped identify and address interface issues.

The concept also included additional service screens, a set of reusable UI components and 18 custom illustrations. It was a high-fidelity concept, not a deployed product.
