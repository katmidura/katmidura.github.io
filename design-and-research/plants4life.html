Plants4Life — Exploring how IoT can support plant care

Overview:

Taking care of plants requires knowledge, time and regular attention. People with different levels of experience face different challenges, and not everyone knows how to deal with them. As a team, we wanted to understand these challenges and explore how IoT could make everyday plant care easier. We combined desk research with interviews to learn more about plant owners' needs, habits and attitudes towards smart plant-care solutions. Based on our findings, we developed recommendations for a potential system called Plants4Life, combining IoT devices with a mobile app. My responsibilities included conducting desk research, interviewing participants and analysing the research findings together with the team.

Context

People take care of plants for different reasons. Some treat it as a hobby, while others work with plants professionally. However, keeping plants healthy requires more than just watering them. It also involves understanding their needs, providing the right amount of light and knowing how to react when something goes wrong. At the same time, IoT offers new possibilities for automating some of these tasks and making plant care more convenient. We wanted to explore how these technologies could support people in their everyday routines and what might prevent them from using smart plant-care solutions.

Problem

People often lack the knowledge and experience needed to take proper care of their plants. Everyday responsibilities and busy lifestyles can also make regular plant-care tasks difficult. Although IoT can help automate some of these activities, people may see smart devices as expensive, complicated or difficult to install. We wanted to understand what plant owners actually need, what challenges they face and how IoT could help make plant care easier and more accessible.

Research

Desk Research

We started by exploring existing materials about the use of IoT in plant care. Our desk research was based on online articles and academic papers describing available IoT solutions, their functionality and their use in everyday life. We also looked at research about interactions between people and plants.

We focused on three main areas:

  • IoT as a way to improve plant care

    • Research showed that automation and remote monitoring could help people take care of their plants, save time and reduce the number of tasks they need to remember.

  • Perceived costs of IoT

    • People may assume that smart devices are expensive. However, our research suggested that the actual costs of some solutions may be lower than people expect.

  • Difficulties for beginners

    • People without previous experience with IoT may find these systems difficult to understand and use. Clear instructions and an easy introduction to the technology could help make it more accessible.

These findings gave us an initial understanding of the possibilities of IoT and helped us prepare for the interviews.

In-depth Interviews

Next, we conducted 10 individual interviews with people who either took care of plants as a hobby or were professionally connected to the plant industry. We divided the interviews between two team members. I conducted five interviews, while the other researcher conducted the remaining five. We used a prepared interview guide and analysed all ten conversations together. Our participants were divided into two groups:

Hobby plant owners:

  1. 6 participants.
  2. 2 men and 4 women.
  3. Ages 20–77.

People connected to the plant industry:

  1. 4 participants.
  2. 2 men and 2 women.
  3. Ages 34–50.

During the interviews, we explored their plant-care habits, everyday difficulties, motivations and experiences with IoT.

Interview Analysis

After completing the interviews, we analysed the responses together. To organise our findings, we divided them into three parts:

  1. Observation: What we heard or noticed during the interviews.
  2. Conclusion: What we understood from these observations.
  3. Recommendation: What could potentially help address the identified needs or problems.

This structure helped us move from individual answers to more general findings and turn them into recommendations for a potential solution.

Key Insights

  1. People need reliable information about plant care

    2. Observation Participants wanted to learn more about plant care when they encountered problems. They often looked for advice and information online.

    Conclusion Access to reliable and easy-to-understand knowledge is important, especially when users are unsure how to handle a particular plant-care problem.

    Recommendation We recommended adding an assistant or a moderated forum to the app, where users could quickly find advice, ask questions and share their experiences.

  2. Users want to reduce the number of plant-care tasks

    3. Observation Many participants preferred to minimise the amount of time and effort spent on routine plant-care activities. Some also had busy lifestyles, which made regular tasks less convenient.

    Conclusion Automating repetitive tasks could make plant care easier and help users manage their plants with less effort.

    Recommendation We recommended introducing smart devices that could automate selected tasks, such as watering and lighting, while allowing users to control them remotely.

  3. Automation and remote control are important advantages of IoT

    4. Observation Participants often mentioned the possibility of automating plant-care processes and controlling devices remotely. Notifications, such as reminders about watering, were also among the features they discussed.

    Conclusion IoT could offer more than just automation. It could help users remember important tasks and monitor their plants without having to check everything manually.

    Recommendation We recommended combining automated devices with a mobile app offering remote control, helpful reminders and clear instructions.

  4. Cost and space can discourage people from using IoT

    5. Observation The most common barriers mentioned by participants were the costs of introducing IoT solutions and the lack of available space for additional devices.

    Conclusion Even when technology could be useful, people might avoid it because they assume it requires expensive or large equipment.

    Recommendation We recommended communicating the actual costs, size and benefits of devices more clearly. Showing how the system works could help people understand that IoT does not necessarily require complicated or expensive equipment.

  5. People need to understand how IoT works before using it

    6. Observation Participants sometimes questioned whether IoT systems would be intuitive and easy to learn. Younger participants were more willing to learn how to use the technology when they saw that it could make everyday tasks easier.

    Conclusion A system can offer useful features, but people may not use them if getting started feels too complicated.

    Recommendation We recommended providing clear instructions and educational materials that explain how the devices work and how users can benefit from them.

Executive Summary

Our research led to three main conclusions:

  1. IoT can make plant care easier

    2. Automation can bring significant benefits, such as saving time and making everyday plant care more efficient.

  2. People need more knowledge about IoT

    3. One of the key factors in making these systems more accessible is educating potential users about available solutions, their benefits and how they work.

  3. Plant owners have different needs

    4. Their expectations vary from basic plant-care support to more advanced features. A potential system should offer flexibility and adapt to different users.

Outcome

Our research helped us identify several challenges faced by plant owners and understand how IoT could potentially support them. We combined desk research and ten interviews to explore users' needs, motivations and concerns. We then organised our findings into observations, conclusions and recommendations. The final outcome was a set of research-based recommendations for Plants4Life, including smart devices, reminders, educational materials and a community forum. The project remained at the research and recommendation stage. We did not design or implement the application or build physical IoT devices.

What could be explored further?

There are several areas that would require further research and validation:

  • Different user needs: We would need to explore whether beginners, hobbyists and professionals require different features and levels of support.
  • IoT adoption: More research could help us understand how much users would actually be willing to spend on smart devices and what might encourage them to try this technology.
  • Notifications: We would need to explore what types of reminders users find helpful and how often they should receive them.
  • Knowledge sharing: A forum or assistant would need further exploration to understand how users prefer to find and share plant-care advice.
  • Ease of use: We would need to check whether people with little experience in technology could understand and use the proposed solutions.

Project Learnings

This project taught me how to combine different research methods to better understand a problem. Desk research provides a starting point: Reviewing articles and academic papers helped us understand existing IoT solutions and prepare for conversations with users. Interviews reveal everyday needs: Talking to people with different levels of experience helped us understand how their habits, knowledge and living conditions influence plant care. Analysis is more than collecting answers: Working with observations, conclusions and recommendations helped me understand how to turn interview findings into useful insights. Research can guide recommendations: I learned how research findings can help identify opportunities for new features and technologies without assuming that every idea will automatically meet users' needs. Overall, this project helped me develop my research skills and understand how to move from desk research and interviews to clear recommendations for a potential digital solution.
