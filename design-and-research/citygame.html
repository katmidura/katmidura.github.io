Kraków City Game — Helping first-year students discover a new city

Overview
Kraków City Game is a location-based service concept designed to help first-year university students discover Kraków beyond its main tourist landmarks. By turning local recommendations into an interactive route with puzzles and challenges, the service connects newcomers with places suggested by residents. My responsibilities included co-organizing and facilitating co-creation workshops, documenting observations, analyzing research findings, and co-developing the service concept and Service Blueprint.
Role
Service Designer / Service Researcher
Timeline
October 2023 – January 2024 (15 weeks)
Tools
Miro, Microsoft Teams
Team
5 Service Designers
Tags
Service Design, Service Research

01 — Context

A new city without a local network

Moving to Kraków for university means adapting to an unfamiliar environment while building a social life from scratch. Although the city offers a wide range of cultural venues, green areas, and local food spots, newcomers often lack an established network to point them toward places worth visiting.

02 — Problem

Scattered information and predictable routes

Major tourist landmarks are easy to find, but discovering less obvious locations, affordable spots, and local events takes effort when information is fragmented across different sources. First-year students wanted a balance between structured guidance and spontaneous exploration, rather than a rigid guided tour.

03 — Process

From local knowledge to a structured service

We combined desk research and two in-depth interviews with new students with two online co-creation workshops in Miro (around six local participants per session). I co-facilitated both workshops, documented observations, and synthesized the findings into design directions.

Decision 1 — iterating the workshop format after a pilot session: During the first pilot workshop, technical issues with Miro, unclear instructions, and repetitive tasks caused participant fatigue. Before the second session, I helped refine the agenda by adding a short Miro onboarding, clarifying task instructions, introducing a break, and streamlining activities to improve engagement and idea quality.

Decision 2 — choosing an interactive city game over events or subscriptions: We evaluated three concepts: a mystery bus, a cultural discount subscription, and a welcome festival. Each faced practical barriers such as capacity limits, existing market competition, or heavy multi-partner coordination. We selected a city game because it combined local recommendations with self-paced exploration and was feasible to design within a 15-week student project.

Decision 3 — mapping backstage operations with a Service Blueprint: To ensure the concept worked beyond the player experience, we co-created a Service Blueprint covering six stages—from partner onboarding and promotion to post-game evaluation. This helped define partner roles (such as providing discounts or preparing staff for visitor traffic) and backstage tasks like QR code setup and user testing.

04 — Solution

A story-driven route powered by local tips

The final concept is a 4–5 hour city game spanning a 5.5 km route across Kraków, built around a narrative where players reconstruct a roommate's forgotten night out using clues in messages and photos.

Using the GooseChase app, participants complete 10 location-based tasks—such as solving riddles, matching photos, and scanning QR codes—at spots like Piwnica Pod Baranami, Manggha Museum, Okrąglak, and Kopiec Krakusa. The service also includes a curated list of additional local spots, partner discounts, and a completion reward, supported by a six-stage Service Blueprint detailing frontstage and backstage operations.

05 — Outcome

A validated service concept ready for field testing

The project delivered a complete service design proposal: research synthesis from interviews and co-creation workshops, a 10-task game route, partner cooperation guidelines, and a comprehensive Service Blueprint.

As an academic project, the city game remained a proposed service concept and was not launched or tested in a real-world environment. Key next steps identified for future iteration include running a live pilot with first-year students, improving route accessibility for players with reduced mobility, and adding personalized paths based on individual interests.
