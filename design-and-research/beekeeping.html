Honey Jar Heating Ring — Making crystallized honey easier to use

Overview
A concept for a targeted heating ring and matching jar set designed to soften crystallized honey for effortless scooping. Developed for beekeepers and their customers, the project explored how a simple physical product could reduce crystallization-related friction without warming an entire jar. My responsibilities included conducting user interviews, analyzing research data, and creating the 3D product models and renders in Blender.
Role
UX Researcher / 3D Modeler
Timeline
October 2025 – January 2026 (15 weeks)
Tools
Google Forms, Blender
Team
4 UX/UI Designers
Tags
UX Research, Product Design, 3D Modeling

01 — Context

Different apiaries, different day-to-day realities

Beekeeping extends far beyond hive maintenance—beekeepers must also process, package, and sell their honey to customers. Because professional beekeepers and hobbyists manage different apiary sizes and workflows, their operational needs vary significantly. Before proposing any design intervention, we needed to understand these differences across the beekeeping process.

02 — Problem

Finding a shared, solvable pain point

Beekeepers face a wide range of challenges, from hive relocation and swarming risks to high equipment costs. Most of these issues require deep technical or agricultural expertise outside our scope as a design team. Our main challenge was identifying a widespread problem affecting both beekeepers and consumers that could be realistically addressed through product design.

03 — Process

From broad field research to a focused product concept

We conducted four interviews with professional and hobbyist beekeepers—I personally led one conversation and collaborated on analyzing all four. Mapping nine distinct problems revealed two recurring themes: finding customers (mentioned by all four participants) and honey crystallization (mentioned by three). To validate how crystallization affects buyers, we surveyed 46 consumers, focusing our analysis on the 36 respondents who actively buy honey.

Decision 1 — narrowing focus to crystallized honey: While finding customers was a universal concern, crystallization directly bridged beekeeper workflows with consumer purchasing behavior. Our survey showed that crystallization discouraged 33.3% of honey buyers from purchasing, giving us a clear opportunity to improve everyday usability.

Decision 2 — leveraging direct-to-consumer relationships: Survey data revealed that 55.6% of honey buyers purchase directly from beekeepers, though 91.6% buy honey only a few times a year or less. Rather than designing a standalone retail gadget, we positioned the product as a starter set that beekeepers could offer directly to their existing customer base.

Decision 3 — heating only the scooping zone: Remelting an entire jar of crystallized honey is time-consuming and unnecessary for daily use. We decided to design a ring that warms only the upper section of the jar where a spoon enters, keeping controls minimal—a power button, battery level dots, and a red-to-green temperature indicator.

04 — Solution

A three-piece set built for everyday scooping

The final concept is a coordinated three-piece set consisting of a cuboid honey jar, a fitted heating ring, and a long spoon designed to reach the bottom of the container. The ring wraps externally around the jar without touching the honey, warming the target layer until the status light turns green.

As my first 3D modeling project, I learned Blender during the process to build the complete product set. I modeled the jar, heating ring, and spoon with accurate proportions and dimensions, and produced final renders showing the ring both standalone and mounted on the jar.

05 — Outcome

A validated research direction and 3D product concept

The project delivered a research-backed product concept supported by beekeeper interviews, a 36-respondent consumer analysis, and detailed 3D visualizations in Blender. It demonstrates how beekeepers could offer an accessible accessory to make crystallized honey easier to use.

Because the project concluded at the digital concept stage, no physical prototype was built or tested. Moving forward, next steps would include prototyping to validate heating speed, battery life, ring ergonomics, and compatibility with standard jar sizes.
