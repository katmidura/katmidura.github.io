Designing a self-service kiosk for university dormitories

Overview:

Living in a university dormitory often means that even simple tasks require help from the reception. Some services are available online, while others can only be handled in person. This can make everyday tasks less convenient for students. As a team, we wanted to explore how technology could improve the dormitory experience and make some of these processes easier. We designed DS Kiosk, a self-service kiosk for students, visitors and other dormitory users. My responsibilities included conducting interviews and observing existing kiosks, designing the overnight stay flow and its hi-fi interface, creating illustrations, and taking part in usability testing.

Tags:

UX/UI, Graphic Design

My role:

UX/UI Designer

Team:

4 UX/UI Designers

Timeline:

March – June 2024 (15 weeks)

Tools:

Figma, Figjam

Context

There were two main ways to handle dormitory-related tasks: through the website or at the reception.

Through the website, students could:

  • Reserve a time slot for doing laundry.
  • Reserve a study room.
  • Buy a parking ID.
  • Report damage.

At the reception, students had to:

  • Inform the receptionist about an expected package.
  • Pay for damage or fines.
  • Collect keys for different facilities.
  • Arrange an overnight stay for visitors.

These two channels were not connected. Some tasks could only be completed in person, while others were available online.

Problem

The lack of connection between these systems made managing everyday dormitory tasks less convenient. There were also differences between the processes in different AGH dormitories. This made it harder for students to remember how to handle each task. At the same time, receptionists had to deal with many residents and their requests, which could make it difficult to communicate information to everyone.We saw an opportunity to make selected dormitory services easier to access without requiring students to visit the reception every time.

Exploration

Interviews

As an initial step, our team spoke with people who had experience with university dormitories. During classes, each team member conducted individual conversations with people from our year. I personally interviewed around two participants. We used informal, open-ended questions rather than a structured interview guide. The conversations helped us explore everyday dormitory tasks and some of the difficulties connected with using reception services.

Some of the topics that came up were:

  • Students wanted an easier way to handle common dormitory-related responsibilities.
  • Tasks such as inviting visitors and handling packages could be inconvenient.
  • Easier payments and faster ways to report problems were among the needs discussed.
  • Having access to useful services at different times was important.

These conversations gave us an initial understanding of the problem. However, because the interviews were informal and involved a small number of participants, we treated them as early observations rather than representative research.

Observing existing kiosks

During this stage, I also noticed an information screen in one of the dormitory buildings. It already displayed useful information for students, but I believed it could have more potential. What if this screen could become a self-service kiosk? To explore this idea, I visited shopping centres and a capsule hotel to look at existing kiosks. I mainly focused on their physical appearance, size and setup. I noticed different forms of self-service devices and used them as inspiration for our design. The capsule hotel was especially interesting because its kiosks were also designed for accommodation-related services. However, its users were short-term guests, while our target users would mostly be long-term dormitory residents. This made me think about how we could adapt the kiosk concept to the needs of a university dormitory.

Key insights

  1. Simplicity and efficiency

    2. We wanted the kiosk to be easy to understand and use. We looked at familiar self-service kiosks, such as those used in restaurants and accommodation facilities, as inspiration for its physical form. Our design would also need additional elements, such as key lockers and a QR code scanner, to support dormitory-related tasks.

  2. Different users and their needs

    3. The kiosk would not be used only by students living in a particular dormitory. It could also be used by residents of other AGH dormitories, family members and visitors.

  3. Balancing user and dormitory needs

    4. We also considered the value of the kiosk for the university and dormitories. One of our ideas was to display advertisements for local events, concerts or career fairs. However, we wanted these to be relevant to residents and not interfere with the main tasks.

Designing the Experience

Lo-fi wireframes

We started by focusing on the overnight stay process, as it was one of the most important functionalities of the kiosk. First, we created a paper version of the user flow together as a team. After agreeing on the main direction, we worked separately on our wireframes. I was responsible for preparing the final overnight stay wireframes in Figma. In my designs, I focused on simplicity and consistency between screens. I considered a few details that could make the process easier to follow:

  1. Visual arrows showed users where to place their card or what action to take.
  2. Users could return to previous steps if they wanted to change something.
  3. Underlined text opened pop-ups with more detailed instructions for users who needed them.

I wanted the interface to guide users through the process without showing too much information at once.

Visual Design

Once our wireframes were ready, we decided to use the visual style of AGH dormitories as inspiration. We wanted the kiosk to feel connected to its surroundings while still having a modern and friendly appearance. Typography: Gilroy Bold and Gilroy Semi-Bold. Color palette:

  1. Background: #FAF9F6
  2. Text: #000000
  3. Primary: #94D4A7
  4. Secondary: #33553D
  5. Accent: #543332

Illustrations

I created a set of 18 illustrations, first on paper and then in Figma. I wanted them to add a friendly character to the interface and make it more visually consistent with the young audience of the dormitories.

Components

We also prepared reusable components to keep the interface consistent across different screens.

Hi-fi Design

DS Kiosk is a touchscreen kiosk concept designed for AGH University of Science and Technology dormitories. It aims to make selected services easier to access for students, visitors and other users.

Lock screen and main screen

The lock screen displays advertisements for local events, such as concerts and career fairs, as well as important information for dormitory residents. For the main screen, we included all six main functionalities in one place. This way, users could quickly find the task they needed without searching through different menus.

Reserving an overnight stay

The overnight stay flow was the main functionality I worked on. One important part of the process was choosing the user's role. Different users followed different paths depending on their relationship with the dormitory. For example, family members and MS residents did not have to pay for their stay, but they needed to go through a more detailed verification process.

Making the process easier with a QR code

We also wanted to reduce the resident's involvement when someone came to visit them. Before: The student had to be present during the whole process and go downstairs to the reception to help their visitor. After: The student could use the DSNet platform to obtain a QR code and send it to the person they were expecting. The visitor could then go directly to the kiosk and scan the code, without the student having to come downstairs. The code could also be reset if it reached an unauthorised person. This was one of the important parts of our concept, as it allowed visitors to complete more of the process independently.

Other functionalities

Besides the overnight stay flow, our team also designed screens for: Handling packages. Collecting keys and a vacuum cleaner. Buying a parking ID. Reporting and paying for damage.

Usability Testing

After preparing the hi-fi prototype, we conducted usability tests with three participants. We asked them to complete the overnight stay flow, which was our main focus, and explore the other available functionalities. During the sessions, we identified several issues that we had not noticed while designing the interface. Based on the feedback and observations, we made the following improvements:

  1. More feedback after purchasing a parking ID

    2. We improved the feedback shown to users after completing the purchase, so they would have a clearer understanding of what happened.

  2. Better visual distinction between buttons and information

    3. Some buttons and informational elements had the same background color, which made them look too similar. We improved their visual distinction.

  3. Adding a document verification screen

    4. We added a separate screen for document verification to make this step clearer in the process.

  4. Removing the back arrow from the final screen

    5. We removed the back arrow from the last screen because it was not needed at that stage of the flow. These changes helped us improve the final prototype based on issues identified during testing.

Outcome

As a result, we created a high-fidelity concept of a self-service kiosk that brought several dormitory-related services together in one place. The project included an overnight stay flow, additional service screens, a visual design system and a set of custom illustrations. The concept also explored how a QR code could make the visitor process more convenient for dormitory residents. However, the project was not without limitations.

What could be improved?

While reviewing the project, I noticed several areas that would need more attention in a future iteration:

  • Some processes may seem obvious to long-term residents but could be confusing for students who have just moved into a dormitory.
  • Users need clear information when they enter incorrect data, such as a wrong number.
  • Not everyone prefers digital solutions. Some students may still feel more comfortable talking to a receptionist or using a paper register.
  • More structured interviews with a wider range of dormitory residents would help us better understand their needs. We would also need to explore the kiosk's functionality in more detail and test it with more users.

Project Learnings

This project taught me how to turn initial observations into design ideas and develop them into a working prototype.

I learned that informal conversations can help identify possible problems, but they are not enough to understand the needs of a whole user group. More structured research would help us make better-informed decisions.

A process that seems simple to one person may be confusing to another. It is important to consider different levels of experience and different user needs.

Some interface issues only became clear when other people tried to use the prototype. Testing helped us improve the design instead of relying only on our own assumptions.

I also learned how to create custom illustrations and use them to make an interface feel more consistent.

Looking back, I would spend more time exploring user needs and validating the idea of a kiosk before moving on to the final design. I would also test the solution with a wider range of residents and visitors.
